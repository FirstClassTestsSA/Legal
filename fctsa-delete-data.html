Delete Your Data

Firearm Competency Test SA

Last updated: 25 November 2026

In short: This app has no user accounts and no sign-in. If you bought the unlock, you can ask us by email to delete your purchase record, which RevenueCat keeps for us; we will do so within 30 days. After that, your paid content may lock on your phone until you tap "Restore previous purchase". Google keeps its own record of the transaction, which we can see in Play Console (Google's website for app publishers) but cannot delete. Expo, which delivers our content updates, keeps update-check records for us that we cannot match to you ourselves; on request we will ask Expo about them (see section 4).

1. Scope

This page explains how to request deletion of personal information processed by Firearm Competency Test SA (Google Play package za.co.fctsa.firearms), published by First Class Tests SA ("we", "us"). For the purposes of the Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013 (POPIA), we are the responsible party (the one who decides why and how your information is used) for this limited processing.

The app has no user accounts and no login. There is no account to delete. A deletion request therefore concerns your purchase record and, if you ask, Expo's update-check records (section 4).

2. How to request deletion

Send us an email with the details below and we will locate and delete your purchase record.

  1. Email fctsa.apps@gmail.com.
  2. Use the subject line Data deletion request.
  3. Send it from the Google account you used to buy the unlock, which helps us verify the request.
  4. Include your approximate purchase date and, if you have it, your Google Play order ID (it starts with GPA. and appears in your Google Play order confirmation email or receipt).

Processing time: we will action your request and confirm by reply within 30 days. We do not charge for this.

3. What we delete, and what happens afterwards

On request, we permanently delete the purchase / entitlement record held by RevenueCat for your purchase. It holds an anonymous identifier that the app created (it contains no name or email address), your purchase and entitlement records (including your Google Play order number), your device's country (from the store), and technical details such as the app version, the RevenueCat software version, the operating-system version and the device model.

Afterwards: deleting the record does not cancel your Google Play purchase. The app checks your unlock with RevenueCat each time it starts online, so the next time it does, RevenueCat may create a new, empty record for the same identifier and your paid content may lock on that phone. To unlock it again, tap "Restore previous purchase" in the app; this creates a new record holding your purchase. While the app stays installed and is used online, a record exists for it again (without your purchase unless you restore it); uninstall the app first if you do not want that.

Every installation, including ones that never bought anything, has an anonymous RevenueCat record, created the first time the app goes online. It holds no name or contact details. The app does not show its identifier and there is no order to look it up by, so we cannot find it.

What Google shows us. In Play Console, Google shows us a record of each purchase: the order number, date, product and price, your country and, for some sales, your state, city and postcode, and the device model. It also lets us find an order by the buyer's email address. We use this only for refunds, support, deletion requests and tax records, and we cannot delete Google's copy (see section 4). When you email us, we also hold your email address and whatever your message contains, only for as long as we need to deal with your request.

Your study progress, quiz results and bookmarks are kept on your device and are never sent to us. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage, removes them from your phone; no request to us is needed. If Android backup is switched on for your Google account, a copy is also kept in your Google account's device backup. Google lets you delete only the whole device backup (all apps and settings), not this app's part of it.

4. What is kept by Google and Expo

Google Play (Google) keeps its own record of the transaction — including your payment and order history — and retains it for legal, financial, and tax-compliance reasons. This is Google's retention under Google's policies, not ours, and we cannot delete it on your behalf. You can review the data tied to your Google account, and the controls available to you, at myaccount.google.com/data-and-privacy.

Expo delivers our content updates. Each time the app is opened fresh, it asks Expo whether new question content is available. The check carries a random installation identifier that the app creates the first time it runs, together with technical labels such as the platform, the app's runtime version and update channel, the identifiers of the content built into the app and of the content currently running, and a user-agent string. Like any internet request, it also reveals your IP address to Expo. If a version of the app's content has failed to start on your phone, later checks also list the failed versions' identifiers; if the update system itself fails at start-up, the next check carries a short technical error message, once.

Expo stores the installation identifier, your IP address and any failed-update identifiers with its records, on our behalf, to roll out updates, count installations and failed updates, and measure use of its service. Expo states that it deletes the start-up error text after three days; it has not published how long it keeps the rest. Because the app does not show you the installation identifier, we cannot look these records up ourselves: contact us and we will ask Expo, which keeps them for us, whether it can find and delete them.

Clearing the app's storage makes the app create new identifiers for Expo and RevenueCat the next time it starts. This does not delete the records Expo or RevenueCat already hold; it only means future checks use new identifiers, and records deleted on request start again the next time the app goes online. Clearing storage also erases your study progress, bookmarks and any free questions or mock-exam credit earned by sharing; a purchased unlock comes back automatically the next time the app is online. Uninstalling and reinstalling may not give new identifiers: if Android backup is switched on, Android restores the app's saved data, including the old identifiers, when the app is reinstalled. If you have bought the unlock, RevenueCat's software also keeps a copy of your RevenueCat identifier with Google Play services on your phone; if Android backup is switched on, Google keeps that copy when the app is uninstalled and restores it to a new phone set up from your backup. When you restore or buy the unlock, the app sends that earlier identifier to RevenueCat, which links the old and new records. Expo describes its update-check data at docs.expo.dev/eas-update/store-privacy-declarations; its general privacy policy is at expo.dev/privacy.
